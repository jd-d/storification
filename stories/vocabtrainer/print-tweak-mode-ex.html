The whole idea

The aorist looks scary because Greek has several ways to build it. The trick is not to guess the whole form. First split the word into clues.

Main question: what tells me the aorist type: stem, sigma, missing sigma, or special stem?

Start here

1. The first-move checklist

Do not start by translating every word. Start by finding the verb shape. In Greek, the verb carries a lot of the sentence.

Verb puzzle method 🔎

  1. Find the verb.
  2. Is there an augment ἐ- near the start?
  3. Look for a stem clue: new stem, sigma, lengthened vowel, or special stem.
  4. Separate: prefix + augment + stem + ending.
  5. Decide: indicative, imperative, or infinitive.
  6. Translate the verb first, then the sentence.

The basic split

prefix | augment | aorist stem | ending ἀπ | έ | κτειν | α συν | έ | λεξ | α

The augment belongs to past indicative forms. Imperatives and infinitives normally do not take an augment.

Concept

2. What is the aorist?

The aorist is a Greek verb system often used for a single, completed, or bounded action. In the indicative, it normally translates as a past action: “I did”, “he went”, “they saw”.

Outside the indicative, especially in imperatives and infinitives, the aorist is mainly about aspect: treat the action as one complete event.

Memory hook:
Aorist = one clean snapshot. Praesens/imperfectum = action as ongoing, repeated, or general.
StemForm typeAspect clueExample idea
praesensstampraesenssituation is going onΤὸ ἔργον πράττω. I do the task.
praesensstamimperfectumsituation was going onΤὸ ἔργον ἔπραττον. I was doing the task.
aoriststamindicative aoristcompleted or bounded eventΤὸ ἔργον ἔπραξα. I did the task.
aoriststamimperative aoristconcrete commandΠρᾶξον τὸ ἔργον. Do the task now.
Common trap: an aorist infinitive or imperative does not automatically mean “past”. It usually means “do the action as one complete thing”.
Type 1

3. Thematic aorist: new stem, familiar endings

Pattern

The thematic aorist uses an aorist stem that may look quite different from the praesens stem. The indicative endings are like the imperfectum endings.

augment + aorist stem + imperfect-type ending ἔ + λαβ + ον ἔλαβον = I took / received

Example: λαμβάνω

2 evἔλαβεςyou took

1 mvἐλάβομενwe took
2 mvἐλάβετεyou took

  3. Translate only the verb first: ἔπαθον, ἔλιπον, εἶδον.
Aspect in commands

4. Aorist imperative and infinitive

Praesens stem

Use the praesens stem when the action is general, repeated, ongoing, or not presented as one finished event.

TypeFormIdea
imperative evλαμβάνεtake / keep taking
imperative mvλαμβάνετεtake / keep taking
infinitiveλαμβάνεινto take

Aorist stem

Use the aorist stem when the action is concrete, single, or treated as completed.

TypeFormIdea
imperative evλαβέtake it once
imperative mvλάβετεtake it once
infinitiveλαβεῖνto take as one action

Textbook-style contrast

Μὴ λείπετε τέκνα.
Do not leave children behind. General or repeated idea.

Εἰπέ μοι τὸ ὄνομα τῆς ἀδελφῆς σου.
Tell me the name of your sister. One concrete act of telling.

Ὁ παῖς ἤθελε σφαῖρας βάλλειν.
The child wanted to throw balls. General activity.

Ὁ Ἀχιλλεὺς ἤθελε τυχεῖν τοῦ Ἕκτορος τῷ δόρατι.
Achilles wanted to hit Hector with his spear. One target, one concrete result.

Common trap: do not translate λαβεῖν as “he took”. It is an infinitive: “to take”. The aorist tells you the action is viewed as complete.

Mini exercise: choose the better aspect

  1. “Tell me your name.” Choose λέγειν or εἰπεῖν. Explain the clue.
  2. “The child wanted to throw balls.” Choose βάλλειν or βαλεῖν. Explain the clue.
  3. Underline the infinitive or imperative: λαβέ, λαβεῖν, ἔλαβον, λαμβάνετε.
Type 2

5. Sigmatic aorist: the sigma clue

Pattern

The sigmatic aorist builds the aorist stem with σ. It has its own endings.

augment + stem + σ + ending ἔ + λυ + σ + α ἔλυσα = I loosened / released

The textbook treats the aorist stem as the stem including sigma: λυσ-.

Example: λύω

TypeForms
indicativeἔλυσα, ἔλυσας, ἔλυσε(ν), ἐλύσαμεν, ἐλύσατε, ἔλυσαν
imperativeλῦσον ev, λύσατε mv
infinitiveλῦσαι

What happens before sigma?

Praesensstam ends in...Aorist clueExamples
-ι, -υ or diphthongadd -σλύω -> λυσ-, πιστεύω -> πιστευσ-
contract stem -α, -εlengthen vowel before sigmaνικάω -> νικησ-, ποιέω -> ποιησ-
π, β, φbecomes ψπέμπω -> πεμψ-, γράφω -> γραψ-
κ, γ, χbecomes ξδιώκω -> διωξ-, ἄρχω -> ἀρξ-
δ, ζ, θconsonant drops, sigma remainsψεύδω -> ψευσ-, νομίζω -> νομισ-
Recognition clue: if you see -σα, -σας, -σε, -σαμεν, -σατε, -σαν, think sigmatic aorist first.

Mini exercise: sigma detective

  1. Underline the sigma clue in ἔλυσας, ἐφυλάξαμεν, ἐπέμψαν.
  2. Predict the aorist stem: γράφω, διώκω, ψεύδω, ποιέω.
  3. Separate prefix, augment, stem, ending: συνέλεξα.
Type 3

6. Pseudosigmatic aorist: sigma is hiding

The pseudosigmatic aorist occurs especially with stems ending in λ, μ, ν, ρ. It has the same endings as the sigmatic aorist, but the sigma is not visible.

The biggest clue is often a lengthened stem vowel.

Name trick:
pseudo-sigmatic = “as if sigma was involved”, but you do not see the sigma in the final form.
ChangeExample stemAorist clue
α(ι) becomes ηφαίνωφην-, as in ἔφηνα
ε stays/appears as εινέμωνειμ-, as in ἔνειμα
short ι or υ becomes longκρίνω, ἀμύνωlonger vowel before the ending
double consonant loses one consonantἀγγέλλωἀγγελ-, as in ἤγγειλα

Example: φαίνω

TypeForms
indicativeἔφηνα, ἔφηνας, ἔφηνε(ν), ἐφήναμεν, ἐφήνατε, ἔφηναν
imperativeφῆνον ev, φήνατε mv
infinitiveφῆναι
Common trap: “no visible sigma” does not automatically mean thematic aorist. If the endings look sigmatic and the stem vowel is lengthened, test pseudosigmatic.

Mini exercise: hidden sigma

  1. In ἔνειμεν, find the augment, stem, and ending.
  2. Sort: ἔφηνα, ἔλαβον, ἔλυσα, ἤγγειλαν.
  3. Explain why φῆναι is not an indicative past form.
Type 4

7. Stamaorist: special stem, learn it as a set

Pattern

The stamaorist does not follow the normal thematic or sigmatic pattern. If a verb has a stamaorist, learn the aorist stem separately in the word list.

special aorist stem + special endings ἔ-βη-ν = I went / stepped ἔ-γνω-ν = I understood / knew

Example pair

Recognition clue: if the form looks short, strong, and does not show a normal thematic/sigma pattern, check the stamaorist list.

Mini exercise: stamaorist

  1. Translate the verb only: ἔβησαν, γνῶτε, βῆναι.
  2. Mark each form as indicative, imperative, or infinitive.
  3. Explain why γνῶτε is not automatically past.
Mixed recognition

8. Four aorist types side by side

TypeMain clueExampleWhat to do
thematicnew aorist stem, no sigmaἔδραμονlearn stem from list: δραμ-
sigmaticvisible sigma or sigma soundφύλαξαιlook for σ / ξ / ψ
pseudosigmaticsigmatic endings, no visible sigmaἤγγειλανlook for lengthened vowel or liquid/nasal stem
stamaoristspecial stemγνῶτεlearn as its own aorist pattern

Fast sorting questions

  1. Is there a clear σ, ξ, or ψ? Try sigmatic.
  2. No sigma, but sigmatic endings and vowel lengthening? Try pseudosigmatic.
  3. No sigma and a very different stem? Try thematic.
  4. Very special short stem like βη- or γνω-? Try stamaorist.

Trap forms

ἔλαβον can be 1 ev or 3 mv. The sentence context decides.

λάβετε can look like a normal plural ending. Check whether it is indicative or imperative from context.

γνῶτε is an imperative form here, not a normal past-tense form.

Mini exercise: sort the forms

  1. Sort into four boxes: ἔπαθον, ἔβησαν, λίπετε, ἤλπισε, ηὗρον, ἤγγειλαν, ἐκέλευσα, φῆνον, εἶλον.
  2. For each one, write the clue that gave away the type.

Schema work is not copying. It is slow-motion recognition. The columns force you to prove how you know the form.

Translation method

10. From verb to sentence

Sentence puzzle method

  1. Find the persoonsvorm or main verb structure.
  2. Parse the verb: person, number, form type, aspect/tense.
  3. Find the subject. Check number and context.
  4. Find objects or complements.
  5. Translate the verb first, then build a natural Dutch or English sentence.
  6. If there is an infinitive after “wanted”, “ordered”, “was allowed”, translate the infinitive carefully.

Example 1

Μετὰ τῶν ἀδελφῶν τάχα εἰς τὸ πεδίον ἐγγὺς τοῦ ποταμοῦ ἔβην.

Verb first: ἔβην = stamaorist, 1 ev, “I went / stepped”.

Then build: with the brothers, soon, into the plain, near the river.

Example 2

Ὦ ἄγγελοι, βῆτε εἰς τὴν ἀγορὰν καὶ ἀγγείλατε τὴν νίκην τῷ δήμῳ.

Verb first: βῆτε = aorist imperative mv, “go!” ἀγγείλατε = aorist imperative mv, “announce!”

Then build: messengers, go to the marketplace and announce the victory to the people.

Test survival tip: when a sentence feels long, translate only the verb first. That often gives you the sentence skeleton.

Mini exercise: verb first

  1. In each sentence, underline only the verb forms first.
  2. Mark each as indicative, imperative, or infinitive.
  3. Translate the verb forms before translating the whole sentence.

Final page

13. Final memory page 🧠

1. Thematic: new stem, no sigma, imperfect-style endings.
ἔλαβον, ἔδραμον, ἔπαθον
2. Sigmatic: visible sigma or sigma-result.
ἔλυσα, ἐφύλαξα, ἐπέμψα
3. Pseudosigmatic: sigma system, but sigma is hidden.
ἔφηνα, ἔνειμα, ἤγγειλα
4. Stamaorist: special stem, learn separately.
ἔβην, ἔγνων, βῆναι
The danger question: “What does this whole sentence mean?”

The better question: “What clue can I find first?”
The first clue is usually the verb.
Find the verb, split the pieces, identify the form, then translate.
Best test order: 1. Verb first. 2. Stem clue. 3. Ending clue. 4. Form type. 5. Subject. 6. Natural translation.
